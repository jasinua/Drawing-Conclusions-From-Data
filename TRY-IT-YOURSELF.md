# Try it yourself: the 10 exercises

Open `try-it-yourself.html`. Each exercise has questions with a "Show answer" button.

| # | Exercise | What it is about | Data |
|---|----------|------------------|------|
| 1 | Estimate a probability | Michael's die gives 29 sixes in 100 rolls. Is it fair? Use a simulation. | `die_roll_simulation_data.csv` |
| 2 | Make a statistical inference | 8-hour vs 6-hour school day. Write H₀, Hₐ and read a p-value (α = 0.05). | none |
| 3 | Evaluate a hypothesis | Is the share of students in the easiest classes below 10%? A proportion test. | `school.csv` |
| 4 | t-test on observational data | Does Indy's hitting % beat the team's 30%? A one-sample t-test. | `volleyball.csv` |
| 5 | A different alternative | Do students at a neighboring school eat less fruit and veg than 2.21 servings? A left-tail t-test. | `school.csv` |
| 6 | Draw conclusions from a correlation | Ice cream and drownings. Spot the confounder (temperature). | `icecream1.csv`, `icecream2.csv` |
| 7 | Type I and Type II errors | Classify court-case outcomes: true/false positive/negative. | none |
| 8 | An A/B test's hypotheses | Matthew's two portfolios. Write H₀ and Hₐ for view time and contacts. | none |
| 9 | t-tests on A/B data | Compare mean view time of A vs B. A two-sample t-test (t = 5.64). | `portfolio.csv`, `portfolio_2col.csv` |
| 10 | Errors in A/B results | Oasana's testimonial test (p = 0.104). Which error could we make? | none |

## Order in one line

Probability (1) → hypotheses and p-values (2) → one-sample tests (3–5) → correlation and confounders (6) → error types (7) → A/B testing (8–10).
